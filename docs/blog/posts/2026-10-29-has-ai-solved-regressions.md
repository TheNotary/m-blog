# AI Has Solved Software Regressions

It is impossible for commercially maintained, vibe-coded slop to have regressions because such products lack the baseline level of stablity required by the label `regression`.

<!-- more -->

I'm finding myself locked in a cycle of being 3 more prompts away from migrating off one piece of vibe-coded software onto my own.  And it sucks.  Buggy software and the gleam of the "Agentic Era" have this way of suckering us into thinking that software took a mere moment of someone's time, and could be replicated in short order.  

But in fact, I can attest that, no, it cost someone else either a swarm of tokens (and a working product to replicate in exact precision, mistakes-and-all) or more likely (maybe?) a lot of someone's time who just wants to go hang out with their family.  They spent their work day on it (presumably) and maybe stayed a little late here and there.  And the software that's been built is mostly good except the part you need which happened to turn out to be utter (rediculous) trash.  

Don't let that fool you again! That rediculous trash heap cost a fortune and you could be doing something intrinsically rewarding like 3D printing a mechanically animated parrot that says "clusterf*k" whenever you ask it to say "cookie."  What a gag that would be.  That could be you, the person you're imagining showing off the parrot to a house guest, pretending you have no idea why it's cursing.  

But no, you're intent on typing into this blog while an LLM renders a bunch of text as a traversible tree because you've fallen for the slop-is-easy trick.

Man... it's still churning away...  what else goes into a blog?  Oh, I realized using the crustiest possible LLMs to write software feels very similar to actually writing software in a refreshing way.  

There's a meme floating around where scientists proved brains are less active when using LLMs vs when actually doing work that requires thinking.   I've been mixed on that, because obviously if you're only asking the person to complete work that requires no thinking, then yeah that's going to show up in the brain scans.  And when the economy was better (**rolls eyes**) and I used to not even think twice about whether I needed to hose over a thousand dollars a month at the AI vendor, I used my brain a lot more than I did before, honestly, just keying in the names of software libraries into google.  Anyway, that was my counter-point until I was instructed to read crusty af LLM outputs, and let me tell you, my brain is still recovering from having to read through all that ... just... nonesesical attempts at articulation.  

Oh finally, it dinged... just 3 more prompts to go.
